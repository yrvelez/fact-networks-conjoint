No debate visible in the retrieved works. Individual US adult panel respondents (N ≈ 3,200 total; ~1,600 per accuracy-salience arm). Each respondent completes the full within-subject design, so the unit of analysis for the primary contrast is the respondent, with headline-level observations nested within respondents. Exposure: Accuracy-salience prompt (binary, between-respondent randomization): in the prompt arm, before each sharing decision the respondent sees the instruction 'Before you decide, consider whether this claim is accurate.' In the control arm, no such instruction appears. The prompt is the manipulated variable; its variation comes from random assignment at the respondent level. Within each arm, the order in which plausibility and sharing blocks appear is counterbalanced (plausibility-first vs sharing-first, randomized per respondent) to separate the two outcome measures from order effects. Outcome: Primary outcome: sharing intention, measured as a binary share/don't-share choice for each headline profile (same as source study). Secondary outcome: plausibility, measured on a 0–100 slider ('How plausible is this claim?') for the same profiles. The key estimand is the interaction between accuracy prompt and information type (false vs true/neutral) on sharing, tested against the null that the prompt does not differentially reduce sharing of false headlines. A secondary estimand is the same interaction on plausibility, expected to be null if the constructs are separable. Identification: Two comparisons do the work. First, within the prompt arm, compare the sharing gap (false minus true/neutral) to the sharing gap in the control arm: if the prompt selectively narrows the misinformation gap on sharing while leaving the plausibility gap unchanged, sharing and plausibility are separable. Second, the counterbalanced block order (plausibility-first vs sharing-first) is used to verify that the sharing–plausibility correlation is not an artifact of measuring one immediately after the other. What must hold: the prompt must not change perceived plausibility (verified by the null interaction on plausibility), and the two block orders must produce equivalent estimates (verified by a pre-specified equivalence test).
